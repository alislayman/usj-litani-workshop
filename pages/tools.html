Tools

What each tool is for, and how to start with it.

Workshop tools

  • Change Marker

    Compare two satellite pictures, mark what changed and date it.

    • In your group's folder it opens your village's pictures, under Our village pictures.
    • Online it holds the tool alone: compare two screenshots of your own under Your own pictures.
    • Press Save work every 15 minutes; Copy for the sheet puts one mark on the clipboard.
    • Open Change Marker
    • Change Marker online

  • Sheet to KMZ

    Turns the group's sheet into one map file for Google Earth.

    • In Google Sheets: File, Download, Microsoft Excel (.xlsx).
    • Drop the file on Sheet to KMZ: it runs on your laptop, nothing is uploaded.
    • Press Save KMZ and open the file in Google Earth: each row with a Shape is on the map.
    • Open Sheet to KMZ

  • Group sheet template

    The sheet every group fills: one row per change, one tab per kind.

    • Your group's own copy is in Google Sheets, linked from its column on the Padlet.
    • Hover over a column title to read what to write; row 2 is a grey example.
    • Download the template (.xlsx)
    • Workshop Padlet folders, sheets, hand-in

Satellite pictures, fires and places

  • Sentinel-2 Explorer Easier to use

    Sentinel-2 pictures of any date, with a swipe and a change view: the easier one to use.

    • No account needed: find the village by name, or open it from the step buttons on your group page.
    • Swipe puts two dates side by side; the short-wave infrared view shows burns.
    • Analyze, then Change, shows where plants were lost between two dates.
    • Open Sentinel-2 Explorer

  • Copernicus Browser

    Every Sentinel-2 picture, any date, any layer: more to learn than Sentinel-2 Explorer.

    • Pick the date in its calendar; a free account opens more tools.
    • Its True color, False color and SWIR layers are Change Marker's three layers.
    • The step buttons on your group page open it on the village and the date.
    • Open Copernicus Browser

  • NASA FIRMS

    Heat spots that satellites saw, with their day and time.

    • Your group page opens it on the village: month by month in step 2, and on each white phosphorus report's day in step 4.
    • In its table choose UTC, then add 3 hours from 2026-03-29 (2 hours before).
    • A heat spot is a pixel 375 m to 1 km wide: it shows where to look, not what burned or why.
    • Open NASA FIRMS

  • EFFIS

    Burnt areas mapped by Copernicus from satellites.

    • Step 2 on your group page opens it on 2026-03-01 to 2026-10-23.
    • Keep the date only: EFFIS states no time zone, and its date is when the area was mapped, which may not be the day the fire started.
    • An empty map may mean the service is down, not that nothing burned.
    • Open EFFIS

  • Google Earth

    Sharp pictures and 3D hills, to match a photo or a video to a place.

    • On the web there is nothing to install; Google Earth Pro is the free desktop program.
    • Tilt the view to see the skyline a camera saw: ridges, buildings, roads.
    • Open a KMZ from Change Marker or Sheet to KMZ to see your marks on it.
    • Open Google Earth
    • Google Earth Pro

  • SunCalc

    Where the sun stood at a place, a date and an hour.

    • Set the place, the date and the Beirut hour a source gives.
    • Shadows point away from the sun: compare them with the footage.
    • Open SunCalc
